A reply that gets an answer has usually done one of a small number of things. Once you can name them, replying stops being a search for something clever and becomes a choice: which of these fits this post?

Below are nine types, with an example of each. The posts and replies are made up for this article. Use them as shapes, not scripts, because a template pasted under real posts is exactly what readers have learned to skip.

## 1. The specific question

Ask for one detail the post left out. Narrow questions get answers. Broad ones get ignored.

> **Post:** We cut our onboarding from 9 steps to 3 and activation went up.

> **Reply:** Which step hurt most to remove? I would guess the team invite.

Offering your own guess makes it easier to answer: the author can simply confirm or correct you.

## 2. The experience

Say what happened when you tried the same thing. This is the reply nobody else can write.

> **Post:** Hot take: founders should do support for the first year.

> **Reply:** Did it for 14 months. The roadmap changed more from support tickets than from any strategy meeting we held.

## 3. The added fact

Bring a number, a source or a date that sharpens the post. Keep it to one, and be sure it is right.

> **Post:** Nobody reads long emails anymore.

> **Reply:** Our longest onboarding email, about 600 words, has the best reply rate of the series. Length was not the problem. The first line was.

## 4. The polite disagreement

Say where the post stops being true, and why. Aim at the claim, never at the person.

> **Post:** Never discount. It trains customers to wait.

> **Reply:** True for subscriptions. For a one-time tool, an annual sale brought in buyers who had sat on the fence for a year, and they did not come back asking for more.

## 5. The missing caveat

Agree, and add the condition under which it works. This is disagreement's friendlier cousin.

> **Post:** Ship every day. Speed wins.

> **Reply:** Works as long as you can roll back in a minute. Without that, daily shipping is daily fear.

## 6. The example

A general post becomes memorable when someone supplies the concrete case.

> **Post:** Good error messages are a feature.

> **Reply:** The best one I have seen: "We could not save this. Your text is safe in your clipboard." It told me what failed and that I had lost nothing.

## 7. The consequence

Take the post one step further. "If that is true, then this follows."

> **Post:** AI makes the first draft of everything free.

> **Reply:** Then editing becomes the skill that is paid for. The people who can tell a good draft from a plausible one get more valuable, not less.

## 8. The on-topic joke

Humor travels well on X, provided it is about the post. A stock joke is a template with a punchline.

> **Post:** Our all-hands ran 90 minutes over.

> **Reply:** At that point it is no longer a meeting. It is a hostage situation with slides.

## 9. The thanks with a detail

Gratitude gets read when it names what helped. It tells the author which part landed, which is useful to them.

> **Post:** A thread on pricing pages, with twelve teardowns.

> **Reply:** The seventh one, with the annual toggle switched on by default, fixed an argument we have had for a month. Thank you.

## How to choose

Read the post and ask three questions in order.

1. **Do I have experience here?** Then use type 2. It is the strongest.
2. **Do I know something the post lacks?** Use 3, 5 or 6.
3. **Do I want to know something?** Use 1.

If the answer to all three is no, consider not replying. Types 4, 7 and 8 need a real opinion or a real joke, and both are obvious when forced.

## What these nine have in common

Every one of them refers to something specific in the post, and every one adds a thing that was not there. That is the whole pattern. A reply that does neither, such as "Great thread!" or "So true", is not on this list because it is not really a reply. It is a like with extra steps.

## Mixing types across a day

If you reply regularly, vary the type. A person who only asks questions looks like they are fishing for attention. A person who only disagrees becomes tiring. A mix reads as someone who is paying attention, and that is the reputation you are trying to build. ## Using a tool without flattening the types

A drafting tool tends to produce whatever is easiest, and the easiest reply is agreement. A good tool pushes the other way. [XAICG](/) has a Question button beside AI Reply for type 1, and tones such as skeptical, witty and supportive that map onto types 4, 8 and 9. For type 2, the experience, no tool can help: take the draft and put in what happened to you.

Then read it once, out loud if you can, and post it yourself.
